# Life Flow — Designing Pookie Around the American Day

**Role:** Life-Flow Researcher · **Date:** 2026-10-11
**Founder's reframe (the entire brief):** *"All modules need to align, be intuitive, the flow of the app must align to solve the day to day struggles of the average American citizen. With or without child, they have mail, shopping lists, to do list, children, their to do items, grades, laundry... the app needs to flow and navigate through their daily life plans."*
**Sources:** AI-MOTHER-VISION.md §1 (the 23-module worry taxonomy), IA-FINAL.md (the shipped 5-tab IA: Garden · Today · Pookie · Grow · Menu)

**Core thesis of this document:** Pookie is not a parenting app with life features bolted on. It is a **life app** whose organizing principle is the human day. The average American opens it for the same reason they check the weather: *tell me what my day is, and help me get through it.* Children are part of the day for some users; for others, the day is theirs alone. The app must feel like it runs *a life* either way.

---

## §1 — The average American's day: the universal rhythm

America runs on a shared daily rhythm, regardless of household composition. Bureau of Labor Statistics time-use data and common sense agree on the shape:

- **5:30–7:30 AM — Wake and survey.** Coffee, news, check the day. The universal question: *"What's today?"* Decisions made here — outfit, breakfast, whether the kid's permission slip got signed, whether the car has gas — determine the day's friction.
- **7:30–9:00 AM — Launch.** Commute, school drop-off, work starts. Timing is everything; the forgotten item (lunch, homework, badge) costs 40 minutes.
- **9:00 AM–12:00 PM — The deep stretch.** Work or housework. This is when bills get paid, appointments get scheduled, errands get plotted — *if* someone reminds you.
- **12:00–1:30 PM — Midday maintenance.** Lunch (packed or bought — the fridge matters), mail arrives, the to-do list either grows or shrinks.
- **1:30–5:00 PM — The afternoon.** Meetings, school pickups, appointments. The day's second crunch: pickups, practices, the pharmacy, the grocery run.
- **5:00–7:30 PM — Dinner and the pivot.** Cook or order; the fridge-emptying problem collides with the tired-brain problem. Homework gets done (or doesn't). Bills, mail, permission slips — whatever piled up during the day demands attention.
- **7:30–9:30 PM — Wind down / second shift.** Laundry, dishes, tomorrow's prep, one last email. Screens, couch, exhaustion.
- **9:30–11:00 PM — Tomorrow preview.** The anxious 10-minute question: *"Is there anything I forgot about tomorrow?"*

### The recurring daily struggles (the real design surface)

These are concrete, universal, and they are what Pookie must absorb:

1. **Mail piles up.** Bills, notices, junk, the school letter that was actually important — a paper stack on the counter that everyone ignores until something is late.
2. **The fridge empties invisibly.** Nobody announces the last milk. Dinner at 6 PM becomes a scavenger hunt or a $42 delivery order.
3. **Laundry accumulates silently.** There is no single day it becomes a crisis; it just becomes a crisis *by Thursday*.
4. **To-dos breed.** Every errand creates two more. Nobody's list survives the week intact because there is no system capturing the *incoming* items (the mail, the school email, the "oh we need that").
5. **Bills lurk.** Autopay covers some, not all. The variable ones — utilities, medical, property tax, the kid's activity fee — surprise people.
6. **Appointments surprise.** The dentist booked six months ago. Picture day. The field trip that needed a signed form *yesterday*. The DMV. All of these arrive with enough lead time; none of them get acted on.
7. **The shopping list is three lists.** Notes app, a text from the spouse, the kid yelling "we're out of cereal" — and nobody claims items, so there are two grocery runs or none.
8. **Money leaves quietly.** Small spending (coffee, drive-through, Amazon) adds up invisibly; the month-end number surprises people who consider themselves frugal.
9. **Nobody sleeps enough.** Screens, stress, and logistics push bedtime later; the next morning's survey is foggy, and the cycle repeats.
10. **The week is planned by no one.** Sunday arrives, Monday launches unplanned; the family runs on reactive mode all week and calls it normal.

Notice: only two of these ten are parenting-specific. Eight belong to *every* American adult.

## §2 — Three days in the life

### Sketch A — Maya, 29, single professional, no kids (Austin, TX)

**6:15 AM.** Maya's phone is her alarm and her first screen. She opens Pookie like she checks weather — habit, 60 seconds. The greeting tells her: today is a normal Tuesday, nothing red. One line: *"Your electric bill ($96.44) is due Friday — approve the payment?"* She approved it last month after Pookie flagged it in a photo of the mail; it's a tap, not a task. Below it: tonight's plan she asked for Sunday — *"chicken stir-fry, 25 min, ingredients are in"* — because the meal plan is already on the shopping list and the shopping got done Monday.

**7:40 AM.** Commute. Pookie stays silent — nothing needs her.

**12:20 PM.** Lunch at her desk. She taps the nutrition what-if: *"what if I eat the burrito bowl?"* — 640 cal, sodium fine, dinner should be lighter. No judgment, one number, back to work. Meanwhile Pookie quietly files today's mail scan she'll do tonight — nothing urgent.

**6:30 PM.** She snaps a photo of the mail on the counter: a medical bill from March (follow-up), a credit-card offer (dismissed), an insurance renewal notice (classified: insurance, due in 45 days, reminder set, not a task today). She drops two items on the shared-with-herself shopping list (paper towels, coffee) because Pookie noticed they're on a 3-week cycle and asked Sunday.

**9:00 PM.** Gym — steps and workout flow in from Health Connect. She glances at the day's closeout: protein on target, 7,400 steps, bill approved, nothing missed. She looks at tomorrow: dentist 8 AM, reminder that the 28th's bill needs approving Friday. She sleeps. Nothing bred.

**What Pookie did for Maya:** ran her mail, her money, her meals, her list, her health — five things that used to live in six apps and a counter pile. She has never once seen a grades module. She does not know they exist. She calls it "the app that runs my life."

### Sketch B — The Nguyen family, two working parents, two kids ages 9 and 12 (suburban Ohio)

**6:05 AM.** Dad opens Pookie while coffee brews. The day lands in one screen: both kids' mornings mapped — *"Liam's field trip today, permission slip signed last night ✓. Ella's picture day Thursday — lay out the shirt tonight."* Below that: his own day — standup at 9, electric bill due Friday approved, groceries half the list claimed by Mom (she's driving past Kroger at 4). One more line: *"Liam's math quiz Friday — he's at 78, the study plan is on his tab."*

**7:20 AM.** Launch chaos. The shared shopping list saves the day: Dad claims the last items on his phone in the parking lot; Mom sees it in real time — one grocery run, no duplicates.

**12:45 PM.** Mom gets a Pookie nudge at work, gentle: *"Spirit week theme tomorrow is 'decades day' — closet check tonight?"* The school email arrived Tuesday; the engine did the lead-time math (3 days) and surfaced it when it's actionable, not when it was sent.

**3:30 PM.** The kids are home; Pookie nudges Liam on his tab: *"Quiz Friday — 10 min of Pookie quiz questions? You skipped yesterday."* Dad gets the aggregate at dinner, not the nag stream.

**6:15 PM.** Dinner is the weekly meal plan's Wednesday dish; Mom snaps today's mail — the water bill (due the 28th, reminder set), a dentist postcard for Ella (appointment suggested against the family calendar, one tap to call), a HOA letter (actionable, drafted response waiting). The 9-year-old added "goldfish crackers" to the shopping list; Pookie deduped it against the existing item and asked once.

**9:45 PM.** Parents do the 60-second tomorrow check together: Thursday — picture day (shirt laid out), quiz Friday (plan active), Mom's late meeting (Dad covers pickup — the shared calendar caught the conflict last week). The fairness view whispers privately: *"Activity spend on Liam is 2.5× Ella this quarter — mostly the robotics fees you chose. Still intentional?"* Not a guilt trip; a mirror.

**What Pookie did:** absorbed the school minutiae (W16), the calendar conflicts (W14-style shared truth), the shopping coordination (W20), the mail (W21), the fairness mirror (W10) — and kept the kids' private spaces private (W8/W17/W22 data the parents never see raw). Nobody opened 23 modules. The day opened the modules.

### Sketch C — A single dad like the founder, one teen (Georgia)

**5:50 AM.** He wakes before the alarm. Pookie's morning line: *"Geometry at 69 — Unit 3 study guide still missing, that's the zero dragging the category. Tonight's plan is already on his tab: 45 min, guide first, then the rework."* Below it: his own day — the Amex dispute status (credit landed), the EVOffer return label (still not sent — reminder set), dentist rescheduling for his son (pending his approval), tonight's dinner plan.

**4:00 PM.** The daily school briefing surfaces in his 4pm slot: check-in cross-check — the teen's check-in says "math is fine," the grade says 69. Pookie's tone is curious, not accusatory: *"His check-in doesn't match the grade — worth one question at dinner?"* The study plan for 6–10 PM is already built from the path-to-90.

**5:30 PM.** Mail photo at the counter: the power bill ($142.18, due the 28th — pay reminder set), a property-tax notice (the big one, 30 days out, calendar event + amount), a solicitation (dismissed). All become tasks or reminders, none become worry.

**7:00 PM.** Dinner, then homework. The teen's Path-to-90 checkboxes are on the class page; he checks two off; they sync to the dad's view. Dad approves the drafted teacher email in the dashboard — exact text he already saw, sent inside the 6 AM–10 PM window.

**9:30 PM.** Tomorrow preview: Geometry rework session continues, dentist call still pending (his approval), drive-supervised hours logged (Georgia's 40-hour requirement — 6 done). The fairness nudge never fires; there's one kid. The teen-health privacy contract holds: mood data is aggregate-only; the dad sees *"steady week"* and nothing more.

**What Pookie did:** ran the school command center (W1), absorbed the mail and the bills (W21/W11), carried the teen's plan (W13 dinners, W15 no — bedtime is a conversation not a lock), and kept the father-son trust intact by design (aggregate-only visibility, no surveillance features anywhere).

## §3 — Fitting the 23 modules into the day's flow

Every module gets: **WHERE** in the day it surfaces, **WHY** there (the real-life trigger), and **WHO** it belongs to. The day is the router; the modules are the destinations the day points at.

### The day's universal core (non-parenting — the childless professional's app)

These modules are **always live**, because the struggles they answer belong to every adult:

| Module | Surfaces when | Why there | Maya (childless) touches it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **W21 Mail intelligence** | **Evening, 6–9 PM** — mail scan ritual at the counter; **morning** — only if something surfaced overnight (bill due ≤3 days, legal) | Mail is a batch process: photographed once daily, surfaced by urgency. Morning silence is a feature — the pile is handled. | **Yes — core.** Her bill photo → pay approval → spend log. This alone is a reason to open daily. |
| **W20 Shopping list** | **Sunday planning** (week list), **throughout the day** (quick-adds), **4–5 PM** ("3 items unclaimed, someone's near Kroger") | The list is a shared state machine, not a page. It surfaces when it changes or when someone is near a store. | **Yes — core.** Her list, her cycle detection ("coffee every 3 weeks — add now?"). |
| **W13 Meal planning** | **Sunday** (week plan builds), **5 PM daily** ("tonight's dish, ingredients are in / missing 2 — claimed?") | The 6 PM dinner crisis is the #1 daily household failure. The plan surfaces before the tired brain decides. | **Yes — core.** Tuesday's stir-fry was decided Sunday; she never "figures out dinner" on a weeknight. |
| **W23 Nutrition** | **Meal times** (what-if, quick log), **evening closeout** ("protein ✓, fiber short — tomorrow adjusted") | Food decisions are made in the moment; the module must be faster than the craving. | **Yes.** The burrito-bowl what-if is a 10-second interaction. |
| **W11 Spend intelligence** | **Quietly, always** — surfaces **Sunday review** ("this week's grocery burn: $142") and **in the moment** (mail bill marked paid → logged) | Nobody wants a finance dashboard at 7 AM. Spend is a weekly mirror + a per-transaction ledger the mail module feeds. | **Yes — core.** The month-end surprise disappears; the dispute credit is tracked. |
| **W7 Banking/documents** | **As needed** — passport expiry 9 months out, allowance day, the document vault when traveling | Documents are a background watch, not a daily surface. Allowance is a weekly rhythm. | **Yes** — passport/ID tracking is hers too. Allowance UX is parent-only; the vault is universal. |
| **W22 Exercise/health tracking** | **Morning** (sleep summary), **evening** (day closeout), **passively all day** (sensor data) | Health is ambient data + two bookends. The module surfaces trends, not numbers. | **Yes.** Steps, sleep, the gentle nudge — this is her quantified life, private to her. |
| **W9 Proactive engine** | **Everywhere, invisibly** — it has no tab; it is the *reason* other modules surface at the right time | W9 is the nervous system, not a surface. Its job is timing: spirit week surfaced at the 3-day lead time, not the send date. | **Yes — the whole point.** Maya's bill approval prompt *is* W9. She never sees "the engine." |
| **W2 Health (adult-facing slice)** | **Morning meds reminder**, **doctor visits** (agenda builder), **annual rhythms** (vaccines, dental) | Medication adherence is the highest-leverage health behavior; the visit agenda is the killer feature. Adult health lives here fully — no child required. | **Yes.** Her meds, her appointments, her labs — a health record that isn't locked inside a pediatric portal. |

### The parenting layer (invisible-but-available for non-parents)

These modules answer the child-worries. **Design rule: for a household with no children configured, every one of these is completely absent** — not greyed out, not a seedling teasing "add a child," absent. No badges, no grove rails, no menu hints. The childless user must never feel they bought half an app. The modules activate *only* when a child profile exists, and even then they surface by the day's logic, not by browsing:

| Module | Surfaces when | Why there |
|---|---|---|
| **W1 School core** | **Morning briefing** (grade deltas overnight), **4–6 PM** (today's plan), **the school-email moment** (teacher reply → draft approval) | Grades change overnight via sync; the plan belongs to the study window; teacher comms have a send window (6 AM–10 PM). |
| **W16 School calendar** | **Morning briefing** (today/tomorrow items), **lead-time nudges** (permission slip: night before; spirit week: 3 days out) | Minutiae is all about lead time — the extractor knows the prep window per item type. Morning is the last chance; lead-time is the first. |
| **W8 Emotional wellbeing** | **Check-in moment** (the 10-second pulse, usually evening), **weekly pattern** (parent digest, aggregate only) | Mood is captured at the day's edge; patterns are a weekly mirror, never a daily alarm. |
| **W17 Friendships** | **Weekly** (birthday reminders 7 days out), **the social-pattern moment** (private to the child) | Birthdays need gift lead time; social health is a slow signal — weekly is the right cadence. |
| **W4 Extracurriculars** | **Season deadlines** (tryout 6 wks → suggestion, 2 wks → nudge, 3 days → alert), **practice days** | Activities are seasonal rhythms, not daily surfaces. The engine escalates only at the deadline ladder. |
| **W12 Childcare** | **Calendar-triggered** (early-release Wednesday → "need coverage 12–3?"), **evening-event detection** | Childcare is a *gap* problem — the module surfaces when the calendar creates a gap, not as a directory to browse. |
| **W14 Co-parenting** | **Handoff windows** (2 hours before pickup — notes template pre-opened), **expense moments** | Custody is a schedule + a ledger; the module lives at the transitions and the receipts. |
| **W15 Screen time** | **Bedtime wind-down** (30-min suggestion), **weekly report** | The daily surface is one gentle nudge; the analysis is weekly. Nagging about screens at 2 PM is how you get tuned out. |
| **W5 College prep** | **Quarterly/semi-annual** (trajectory review), **deadline ladder** (30/14/7/2 days), **age-gated milestones** | College is a multi-year arc, not a daily feed. It surfaces at milestones and deadlines — and stays dormant for the 9-year-old entirely. |
| **W6 Life skills/driving** | **Eligibility moments** (permit countdown), **practice windows** (weekend drive-logging), **the "turning 18" bundle** | Driving is a state machine with a countdown; life skills are event-triggered (job interview next week → mock interview). |
| **W18 Summer** | **February–April** (deadline season), **dormant otherwise** | Summer planning is a 10-week annual window. The module *sleeps* 9 months a year — by design, per the "silence is designed" rule. |
| **W10 Sibling fairness** | **Weekly digest, parent-only** ("spend on A is 3× B — intentional?") | Fairness is a slow mirror. Daily surfacing would be guilt-tripping; weekly is reflection. Single-child families never see it. |
| **W3 Safety** | **Zone transitions** (arrived at school), **SOS** (always reachable, never advertised), **the quiet contract** (teens see the same map) | Safety is an *insurance* module — it should be felt as calm, not seen as features. The daily surface is near-zero by design. |
| **W19 Guardianship** | **Annual review nudge**, **document-stale events** (passport expiring + trip on calendar) | The worry no one says out loud; the module is a vault with an annual heartbeat. |

### The split, stated plainly

- **The childless professional's app (Maya):** W21, W20, W13, W23, W11, W7, W22, W2-adult, W9-as-timing. **Nine modules, zero children, complete life coverage.** She should describe Pookie as "it runs my mail, my money, my meals, my list, my health" — and be right.
- **Parenting-only (14):** W1, W3, W4, W5, W6, W8, W10, W12, W14, W15, W16, W17, W18, W19. All stay **fully invisible** until a child profile exists. When one does, they surface on the day's logic — the morning briefing, lead-time nudges, handoff windows, deadline ladders — never as a module index to browse.
- **The bridge modules (life-first, parenting-extended):** W13 meal planning serves the adult's Tuesday dinner *and* the family's week; W20's list is per-household; W22's privacy is per-metric per-person; W11's ledger tracks both the electric bill and the robotics fees; W7's vault holds her passport and the kid's birth certificate. **These are the proof of the founder's reframe** — one module, one life, children or not.

## §4 — The interconnection map: 10 day-moments

The modules must feel like one life. Each moment below is a **timestamped, cross-module flow** — the kind of thing that makes a user say "how did it know?" The answer is always: *it read the mail, it knows the calendar, it remembers the cycle.*

1. **7:12 AM — Mail → task → money.** The evening's mail scan flagged the electric bill ($142.18, due Friday). Morning briefing: one card — *"Approve payment?"* One tap → W21 marks it planned, W11 logs the spend, the Friday reminder cancels itself. *Modules: W21 → W11 → W9 timing.*
2. **7:40 AM — School email → lead-time task.** Tuesday's teacher email said picture day is Thursday. The W16 extractor turned it into a Wednesday-night prep task (*"lay out the good shirt"*) and a Thursday-morning card. Dad sees it when it's actionable, not when it was sent. *Modules: W1 comms → W16 → W9 timing.*
3. **12:20 PM — What-if → day rebalance.** The burrito bowl is 640 cal and 45% of the day's sodium. W23 adjusts tonight's W13 plan silently (lighter dinner suggestion) and the evening closeout reflects it. No lecture; the numbers just move. *Modules: W23 ↔ W13.*
4. **3:05 PM — Calendar gap → coverage.** The school calendar shows early-release Wednesday (W16). The family calendar has a work conflict (shared truth). Pookie: *"Need coverage 12–3? Maya (sitter) is free — she's rebooked 3 times."* One tap starts the request flow (W12). *Modules: W16 → family calendar → W12 → W11 (the booking lands in the ledger).*
5. **4:47 PM — Store proximity → list claim.** Dad drives past Kroger. Three shopping-list items are unclaimed (W20 knows location). Gentle: *"3 items unclaimed — you're 2 minutes from Kroger."* He claims them; Mom sees it live; the second grocery run dies. *Modules: W20 → location context → W9.*
6. **5:30 PM — Cycle memory → preemptive list.** Milk is on a 9-day purchase cycle (learned from the W20/W11 receipt history). Day 8: *"Milk due tomorrow — add it now?"* One tap. The "we're out of milk" crisis never forms. *Modules: W20 ↔ W11 pattern memory.*
7. **6:15 PM — Meal plan → list → nutrition closeout.** Sunday's W13 plan dropped its grocery list into W20 (tagged "meal plan"). Wednesday's dinner matched the plan; W23 scores reality against it (*"lunch matched, dinner drifted — protein still on target"*). Thursday's plan adjusts. *Modules: W13 → W20 → W23 → W13 (the loop).*
8. **8:40 PM — Sleep debt → mood correlation.** Three nights under 6 hours (W22) coincide with "rough" check-ins (W8). The parent digest says: *"Sleep's been short this week — the lever might be bedtime, not willpower."* The teen sees only the supportive opener. Nobody is accused; the correlation is the insight. *Modules: W22 → W8, privacy contract intact (aggregate to parent, private to child).*
9. **9:45 PM — Tomorrow preview → conflict catch.** The nightly check: picture day Thursday (W16), the 4 PM pickup conflicts with Mom's late meeting (shared calendar) — flagged *last week*, already resolved (Dad covers). The geometry study plan is staged for 6 PM (W1). The dentist call still needs the dad's approval (draft waiting since Monday, inside the send window). *Modules: W16 + W1 + W14-style shared calendar + W9.*
10. **Sunday 8:00 PM — The week build.** The one weekly ritual: W13 proposes the meal week (from W22 activity — tournament Saturday → carb-loading Friday), W20 builds the list from it, W11 shows last week's grocery burn, W16 previews the school week, W15's weekly report lands, the fairness mirror whispers (W10). Twenty minutes; the whole week is staged. *Modules: W13 + W20 + W11 + W16 + W15 + W10 — the week's command center.*

**The design rule these moments imply:** interconnections are *event-driven and time-stamped*, never dashboard widgets. A user should never open Pookie to "check the W13↔W20 integration." They open Pookie at 5:30 PM and the dinner plan already knows the shopping list. **The flows are invisible; only the moments are visible.**

## §5 — The morning ritual, reframed

**"Open Pookie, see your day, handle your life."** The ritual is 60 seconds. It is the same gesture for everyone — one screen, one scroll — but the *contents* differ by life, not by settings page.

### Maya's 60 seconds (childless, 6:15 AM)

1. **The line (3 sec):** *"Good morning. All quiet — one thing could use a look."* (Quiet mornings say *"All quiet. Nothing needs you today"* — and mean it.)
2. **The one thing (20 sec):** The electric-bill approval card — amount, due date, one-tap approve. Below it, the fold: *"Chicken stir-fry tonight, 25 min — ingredients are in."*
3. **The day strip (15 sec):** A horizontal strip — dentist 8 AM tomorrow, bill reminder Friday, nothing today. Scannable, not a calendar grid.
4. **The close (20 sec):** Protein yesterday ✓, 7,400 steps, sleep 6h 50m. She closes the app. Done.

**Total touches:** one screen, one optional tap. She did not visit a module. She handled her life.

### The dad's 60 seconds (parent, 5:50 AM)

1. **The line (3 sec):** *"Good morning. 2 things could use a look — the kids' mornings are mapped."*
2. **The kids' mornings (20 sec):** Liam's field trip (slip signed ✓), Ella's picture day Thursday (shirt tonight). Each is a line, not a module — tapping one would open W16, but he doesn't need to.
3. **The money/life layer (20 sec):** The power bill approved yesterday (W21→W11, done). The EVOffer return label still missing (reminder set). Dinner's plan is staged.
4. **The day strip (15 sec):** Geometry study plan at 6 PM (W1), dentist call pending his approval (draft waiting), pickup covered.
5. **The close:** He closes the app. The day is *handled* — not listed, not organized, handled.

**The ritual contract (both users):**
- **One screen, one scroll, 60 seconds.** If it takes longer, the ritual dies.
- **Silence is the default.** "All quiet" is a valid and celebrated morning — the app must be proud of having nothing to say.
- **Every card is winnable.** One card, one ask, one tap. The approval, the claim, the tonight-reminder — never "go explore the module."
- **The day's logic, not the module's.** Nothing on the morning screen says "W21" or "Mail module." It says *"Electric bill — approve?"* The modules are the engine room; the morning is the bridge.
- **Childless and parent rituals are the same shape.** The difference is *content*, not structure. This is the founder's reframe made concrete: the app doesn't have a "parent mode" and a "single mode." It has a *life*, and the life has or doesn't have children.

## §6 — Navigation implication: what the day-first frame means for the IA

**Status: recommend, don't redesign.** The IA reframe pass comes next; this section is the Life-Flow Researcher's input to it. The shipped IA is 5 tabs (Garden · Today · Pookie · Grow · Menu/Groves). The day-first frame does not break this IA — but it sharpens it in five ways:

### 1. The mental model: the day is the map, the modules are the territory
The current IA already leans this way: Garden is the morning ritual, Today is the attention queue ("what needs me"), Menu is the full module index. **Recommendation: make this explicit in the IA language.** The app should teach one sentence on day one: *"Pookie runs your day. The tabs are just where the pieces live."* Users should never need to learn what a "grove" is to handle their morning — the groves are for *browsing*; the day is for *living*. Keep the garden metaphor for the ritual surface (it earned its place in testing), but the IA copy should speak **day language** on Garden/Today ("This morning," "Tonight," "Tomorrow") and module language only in Menu.

### 2. Tab names: the life-frame suggests small, specific renames
- **Garden** — keep. It's the home, the ritual, the habit machine. But its subtitle/copy should be day-first: not "your garden" but *"your day, tended."*
- **Today** — keep the name; it's already the right word. Strengthen the contract: Today is *the day's* engine (Now / This Week / Coming Up), and W9 lives here as timing, never as a named feature.
- **Pookie** — keep. The AI clearing serves both lives (Maya asks "what's for dinner given my week?"; the dad asks "draft the teacher email").
- **Grow** — keep for parents (rings, streaks, fairness reflection). **For the childless user, Grow must not feel like an empty parenting trophy case.** Recommendation: Grow shows *her* growth — health trends, streaks, money patterns, the week's wins bouquet. The IA already supports this (rings are per-person); the content plan must commit to it.
- **Menu → Groves** — the gate review already decided this rename. The life-frame adds one requirement: the grove map must be **life-grouped, not module-grouped** for first comprehension. The 5 groves exist; the test is whether Maya can find "mail" and "money" without learning the taxonomy. Plain parent-verbs already do this ("Mail," "Shopping List," "Meals"); hold that line.

### 3. The home screen: the morning ritual IS the home screen
IA-FINAL's Garden hub (GreetingHero → AttentionQueue → GroveSections → TodayStrip) already *is* the §5 ritual. The life-frame's addition: **the AttentionQueue must be day-ordered, not module-ordered.** The queue's ranking should read like the sketches: bill approval (money, today) → shirt tonight (school, tonight) → pickup coverage (calendar, tomorrow). A user should be able to narrate their day from the queue order. If the queue ever reads as "3 mail items, 2 school items," the day-frame has lost.

### 4. The childless user's IA: absence, not adaptation
The strongest navigation recommendation in this document: **no "parenting" affordance may be visible to a childless household.** Not a greyed grove, not a seedling that says "add a child to unlock," not a Grow tab full of child rings. The Menu grove map for Maya shows her nine life modules; the parenting groves simply don't render. When she adds a child (life changes — this is a *life* app), the groves grow. The IA's "taking-root seedling" pattern is for *unbuilt* modules, never for *not-yours* modules. **The app must feel complete at every household size.**

### 5. The mental model to carry into the reframe pass
One sentence for the IA team: ***"The user lives in the day; the modules live in the app."*** Garden and Today are the day. Menu/Groves is the app. Pookie is the voice. Grow is the memory. Every IA decision in the reframe pass should be tested against the two 60-second rituals in §5 and the three sketches in §2: *can Maya handle her Tuesday without learning a single module name? Can the dad handle his Thursday without opening more than the home screen?* If yes, the IA holds. If the answer requires the Menu tab, the day-frame has leaked.
